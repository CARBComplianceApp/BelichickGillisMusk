# How to Read Your CARB Clean Truck Check Pass/Fail Report

A Clean Truck Check result is not a shop invoice. It is the record CARB and DMV use to decide whether that VIN may keep operating on California roads. Two test paths exist. The report looks different on each path. The consequence is the same: pass, fail, or Not Ready.

Official program pages: [CARB testing requirements](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/clean-truck-check-emissions-compliance-testing-requirements) · [OBD readiness criteria](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/clean-truck-check-board-diagnostic-obd-readiness-criteria).

## Two reports, two kits

| Path | Who takes it | What the report shows |
|---|---|---|
| **OBD** | 2013+ diesel and 2018+ alternative-fuel engines | MIL status, DTCs, readiness monitors, warm-up cycles, miles/minutes since codes cleared |
| **OVI** | Typically 2012-and-older diesel (non-OBD) | SAE J1667 snap-acceleration opacity % plus visual inspection of emissions controls |

Comparison: [OBD vs OVI for fleets](https://www.norcalcarbmobile.com/blog/obd-vs-ovi-clean-truck-check-fleets). Yard process: [How mobile testing works](https://www.norcalcarbmobile.com/blog/how-mobile-carb-testing-works).

Find the official result in the owner’s **CTC-VIS** account at cleantruckcheck.arb.ca.gov. The tester upload is not a substitute for checking the portal.

## How to read an OBD result

**Pass** means no disqualifying fault is present and the vehicle met readiness. **Fail** means an emissions-related fault is active in a way the program rejects. **Not Ready** is not a fail. It means the OBD system has not completed enough operation since codes were cleared, the battery was disconnected, or a scan tool reset the monitors.

CARB current readiness rule (confirm against the live CARB page before quoting in a dispute):

- No DTCs / permanent DTCs, **and** at least one of: 5 warm-up cycles since codes cleared, 200 miles SCC, 400 minutes engine run time SCC, or at most two incomplete monitors.
- If a permanent DTC is present, it can be ignored only if the vehicle has at least 15 warm-up cycles SCC, 400 miles SCC, 800 minutes run time SCC, or at most one incomplete monitor.

A warm-up cycle is not a key-on. Coolant must rise at least 40°F after start and reach at least 140°F on diesel (160°F on natural gas).

Do not clear codes the night before the appointment. That is the most common way a good truck becomes Not Ready and burns part of the 90-day window.

## How to read an OVI / opacity result

Non-OBD units take a SAE J1667 snap-acceleration smoke test plus a visual inspection of required emissions control equipment. The number on the report is percent opacity after the procedure’s ambient correction.

Use the current California HD I/M cutpoints in **13 CCR § 2196.6**, not the old 40/55 PSIP table:

| Engine / equipment | Opacity must not exceed |
|---|---|
| 2007 and newer diesel | **5%** |
| Any vehicle with a Level 3 VDECS (DPF-class) | **5%** |
| Level 2 VDECS, or 1997–2006 diesel | **20%** |
| 1991–1996 diesel | **30%** |
| Pre-1991 diesel | **40%** |

A visual fail (missing, damaged, or tampered aftertreatment) fails the inspection even if the opacity number looks low. Tampering is not a retest-and-hope problem.

## What pass and fail mean for registration

A **pass** uploaded inside the allowed window satisfies the testing half of that deadline. The CARB annual fee is separate and is paid in CTC-VIS.

A **fail** does not disappear if you keep driving. Clear the fault, repair the aftertreatment, then retest. A late or missing demonstration can lead to enforcement action and a **DMV registration hold**.

A **Not Ready** result does not count as the required passing test. Drive the truck through the readiness thresholds, then test again. Do not keep resetting the ECM.

Cadence after a pass: most commercial units book the next window at **+17 weeks**. Ag and CA rec/emergency motorhomes stay annual. Deadlines: [2026 testing deadlines](https://www.norcalcarbmobile.com/blog/2026-carb-testing-deadlines).

## After a fail — four steps

1. Read the actual codes or opacity number. Do not guess from the check-engine light.
2. Repair the emissions system. Common field fails: DEF / SCR faults, DPF soot or missing regen, sensors left unplugged, codes cleared with no drive cycle, visible tampering.
3. Confirm readiness before the retest. If the shop just cleared codes, the truck is not ready.
4. Retest and confirm the new result in CTC-VIS. If it is not uploaded, it did not happen.
